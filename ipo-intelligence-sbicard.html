Read on structure: Of the ₹10,340.79 Cr offer, only ₹499.25 Cr (4.8%) was a Fresh Issue — the rest, ₹9,854.77 Cr (95.2%), was SBI selling down stake via Offer for Sale. Almost none of the IPO proceeds funded the business directly; the deal was overwhelmingly an exit event for the promoter, not a capital raise. That is a structural fact worth weighing independent of the growth story.

Basic EPS
Growth quality: Revenue grew from ₹347.10 Cr (FY17) to ₹728.68 Cr (FY19) — a 44.9% 2-year CAGR — while PAT compounded even faster at 52.1%, meaning margins expanded as the book scaled. The 9-month FY20 PAT of ₹1,161.21 Cr already exceeded all of FY19, confirming growth was accelerating into the listing.
Cash flow caveat: The Prospectus discloses the company has had negative operating cash flow in the past (Risk Factor #46) — typical for a fast-growing lender funding receivables with borrowings before collecting repayments, but it means reported profit is not the same as cash generation. Total borrowings stood at ₹18,577.69 Cr as of Dec 2019, more than 3.8x net worth.

Bull · Base · Bear (illustrative scenario framing, not a price target)
Bull Case

Card spends and receivables keep compounding above 35-40% as India's card penetration rises from a low base; RoNW stabilises near 24%; the market treats the stock as a scarce, high-quality compounder with no listed peer. Multiple holds or expands.

Base Case

Growth normalises toward 25-30% as the book matures; RoNW gently compresses as capital base grows faster than profit (already visible: 26% to 25% to 24% FY17-19); multiple mean-reverts toward the 45-60x range implied by run-rate earnings.

Bear Case

Unsecured lending losses rise in a downturn (no collateral backs the card book), funding costs increase, RBI tightens capital or rate rules, and the 80x trailing multiple compresses sharply — the classic "priced for perfection" unwind.
